# Decision-stage literature check — 21 October 2026

The original directional question and the proposed risk pivot both have substantial prior literature. This is a targeted public-source check, not proof of novelty or an exhaustive review.

- [Lu, Reinert and Cucuringu, Trade Co-occurrence, Trade Flow Decomposition, and Conditional Order Imbalance](https://doi.org/10.1080/14697688.2024.2358963): next-day market-excess returns and portfolio strategies already appear here. Our relative-return extension is an appropriate control and snapshot adaptation, not a novel objective. Trade-co-occurrence classifications cannot be reconstructed from these minute book snapshots.
- [Chordia, Roll and Subrahmanyam, Order Imbalance, Liquidity, and Market Returns](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/7gh9t9w3): already examines next-day liquidity and volatility predictability. In the cited working-paper version, imbalance's next-day volatility contribution disappears with liquidity/volume controls and does not predict next-day spread changes. Strong lagged liquidity/volatility controls are essential for our risk comparison. Their trade imbalance differs from our queue state.
- [Bogousslavsky and Collin-Dufresne, Liquidity, Volume, and Order Imbalance Volatility](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3336171), Journal of Finance 2023: imbalance volatility and liquidity/inventory risk are established research themes. Our daily queue variability is not automatically a new mechanism.
- [Jain, Jain and McInish, The Predictive Power of Limit Order Book for Future Volatility, Trade Price, and Speed of Trading](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1787625): the abstract reports predictive information in book slope and cost-to-trade measures. Thus “the book predicts volatility” is too broad to claim as an original finding.
- [Fu, Ye and Wang, Shallow versus Deep: The Anomaly in the Limit Order Book](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/7436741.pdf?abstractid=7436741&mirid=1&type=2), September 2026 preprint: the abstract studies instability of depth imbalance, future volatility and weekly returns in Chinese stocks. This is especially relevant to any claim about depth-specific queue-risk prediction. Only the abstract was checked, not the full implementation.

A defensible pitch would need a narrower empirical contribution: for example, a reproducible, incremental, cross-period risk effect from coarse queue snapshots after strong own-history/liquidity controls, with a specific explanation and a comparison to these prior constructions. Even that would be a candidate for Mihai's judgment, not an established novel paper. The negative directional result can still be a useful research update.
