# Novelty check: snapshot OFI aggregation and price-input controls

Checked 21 October 2026. This targeted search is not proof that no prior paper contains the same result. No journal acceptance or unique contribution is established.

| Prior work | What overlaps | Narrow proposed distinction |
|---|---|---|
| [Cont, Kukanov & Stoikov, The Price Impact of Order Book Events](https://arxiv.org/html/1011.6402v3), published 2014 | Defines event contributions and sums them over a window; relates them to contemporaneous price changes. Also discusses sampling-related impact differences. | We compare summing observed minute transitions with applying the operator only to coarser endpoints, on the same underlying paths. This does not challenge correctly aggregated event OFI. |
| [Su, Sun, Li & Yuan, The Price Impact of Generalized Order Flow Imbalance](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.02947), 2021 preprint | Explicitly recognizes missing transitions between snapshots and multi-tick quote moves, proposing a generalized deeper-book construction and evaluating same-window explanatory fit. This is the closest overlap. | Our proposed package is the exact path-error identity, material sign-disagreement audit across 483 US stocks, frozen-size price-input control, and a tested summary that preserves observed increments. We do not claim to invent snapshot-aware OFI or replicate their generalized estimator. |
| [Cont, Cucuringu & Zhang, Cross-impact of order flow imbalance in equity markets](https://arxiv.org/html/2112.13213), published 2023 | Separates contemporaneous and lagged relations and studies integrated multi-level OFI. | Our question concerns temporal endpoint substitution, not cross-asset or cross-level integration. No claim that separating explanation from prediction is new. |

The first two full texts were inspected, including the original operator and the generalized-OFI discussion of limited snapshot intervals. Additional exact-phrase searches included “order flow imbalance” with “coarsening,” “endpoints,” “sampling bias,” “mechanical snapshot,” and “2/pi.” These searches did not identify a directly matching empirical study, but search coverage and terminology are imperfect. Generic data aggregation and sign-transform mathematics are not novel.

The credible contribution, if the empirical results withstand review, is a *measurement and identification audit*. A publishable note would need to show that the failure mode is quantitatively consequential and not merely an obvious implementation mistake. The strongest next external check is whether Mihai knows a direct prior result on this exact endpoint-versus-sum comparison. An event-level benchmark would strengthen the paper but is not required to establish the narrower within-archive coarsening facts; the current data cannot establish error relative to true event flow.
